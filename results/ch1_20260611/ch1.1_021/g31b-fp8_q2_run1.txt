To understand how a Transformer processes a sentence, we must follow the data as it transforms from raw text into a high-dimensional mathematical representation and, finally, back into a probability distribution over a vocabulary.

Here is the step-by-step process.

---

### Phase 1: Input Pre-processing
Before the neural network sees the text, the sentence must be converted into numbers.

**1. Tokenization**
The raw sentence (e.g., *"The cat sat"*) is split into smaller units called **tokens**. Modern transformers use **Subword Tokenization** (like Byte Pair Encoding or WordPiece). 
*   *Example:* "Transformer" might be split into `["Trans", "former"]`.
*   Each token is mapped to a unique integer ID from a pre-defined vocabulary.

**2. Input Embeddings**
The integer IDs are passed through an **Embedding Layer**, which is essentially a lookup table. Each token ID is replaced by a high-dimensional vector (e.g., 512 dimensions). 
*   These vectors are learned during training; tokens with similar meanings are placed closer together in this vector space.

**3. Positional Encoding**
Unlike Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Transformers process all tokens simultaneously (parallelism). They have no inherent sense of word order. To fix this, **Positional Encodings** are added to the embeddings.
*   A vector of the same dimension as the embedding is generated using sine and cosine functions of different frequencies.
*   $\text{Input} = \text{Embedding} + \text{Positional Encoding}$.
*   This ensures the model knows that "The dog bit the man" is different from "The man bit the dog."

---

### Phase 2: The Transformer Block (The Core)
A Transformer consists of a stack of identical layers. Each layer has two primary sub-components: **Multi-Head Self-Attention** and a **Position-wise Feed-Forward Network**.

#### Step A: Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA)
This is where the model determines which other words in the sentence are relevant to the current word.

1.  **Linear Projections (Q, K, V):** For every token vector, the model creates three new vectors by multiplying the input by three learned weight matrices:
    *   **Query ($Q$):** "What am I looking for?"
    *   **Key ($K$):** "What information do I contain?"
    *   **Value ($V$):** "What information do I provide if I am relevant?"
2.  **Calculating Attention Scores:** To find the relationship between token $i$ and token $j$, the model takes the dot product of $Q_i$ and $K_j$.
    *   $\text{Score} = Q \cdot K^T$
3.  **Scaling and Softmax:** The scores are divided by the square root of the dimension of the keys ($\sqrt{d_k}$) for numerical stability, then passed through a **Softmax** function.
    *   This turns the scores into probabilities (summing to 1). A high score means token $i$ should "pay more attention" to token $j$.
4.  **Weighted Sum:** The Softmax scores are multiplied by the Value ($V$) vectors.
    *   $\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$
5.  **Multi-Head Aspect:** This process happens $N$ times in parallel (heads). One head might focus on grammar, another on entity relationships, and another on coreference (e.g., linking "it" to "cat"). The results are concatenated and projected back to the original dimension.

#### Step B: Residual Connections and Layer Normalization
To prevent the "vanishing gradient" problem in deep networks:
*   **Residual Connection:** The original input to the attention layer is added back to the output: $\text{Output} = \text{LayerNorm}(\text{Input} + \text{Attention}(\text{Input}))$.
*   **Layer Normalization:** This stabilizes the mean and variance of the activations.

#### Step C: Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)
After attention has gathered context from other words, the FFN processes each token independently.
1.  The vector passes through a linear layer, then a non-linear activation function (usually **ReLU** or **GELU**), and then another linear layer.
2.  This allows the model to "digest" the information gathered during the attention phase and transform it into a more complex representation.
3.  Another **Residual Connection** and **Layer Normalization** are applied here.
